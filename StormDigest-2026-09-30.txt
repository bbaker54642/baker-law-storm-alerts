--- POST #1 — Cold Front Flooding, SE Texas ---

Flooding is moving across South Texas and Southeast Texas today. The
National Weather Service has issued Flood Watches from San Antonio
through the Brazos Valley. Forecasters are using life-threatening
language for parts of the state. [1] [2]

When flooding and wind hit at the same time, the storm is only the
beginning of the problem.

Most homeowner's policies exclude flood damage. National Flood Insurance
Program policies cover flood but not wind. Carriers know exactly where
those policy lines are. When a storm produces both wind and flood damage,
they draw those lines in ways that favor denial.

Under most NFIP policies, policyholders have 60 days from the date of
loss to file a proof of loss. That window runs from the date of damage,
not from the date the adjuster visits or the estimate arrives. Missing
that deadline is a denial.

Before anything is moved or cleaned, photograph and video your damage
with timestamps. The insurance company's adjuster will document what
they find. You should do the same.

Flood damage and wind damage look different to adjusters. They look the
same to the people whose homes just took six inches of water. The carrier
decides which peril caused which damage. That decision determines which
policy applies.

--- POST #2 — TWIA Rate Filing / October 1 Deadline ---

TWIA filed for no rate increase for 2027. Coastal Texas homeowners
will pay the same premium next year that they pay today. [1]

That is good news. Here is what is behind it.

TWIA's actuaries found the association's rates are now adequate by
about 9% for residential coverage. That adequacy is tied directly to
House Bill 3689, passed during the 2025 legislative session. HB 3689
reduced TWIA's minimum funding requirement from surviving a
one-in-100-year storm to surviving a one-in-50-year storm. That change
cut TWIA's reinsurance costs by roughly 46%. [2]

Rates stayed flat because the funding cushion got smaller.

The Texas Department of Insurance held public meetings on this filing
in Galveston on September 23 and in Corpus Christi on September 27. [3]
Written public comments close tomorrow, October 1. The TDI Commissioner
has until October 15 to approve or disapprove the filing.

TWIA covers roughly 240,000 coastal Texas properties. When a major
hurricane makes landfall on this coast, the adequacy of TWIA's reserves
is not a theoretical question. It is the question that determines
whether claims get paid in full.
