TWIA BOARD MEETING — TOMORROW, OCTOBER 8, 2026 (9:30 AM CT)

Full agenda confirmed. TWIA Board will vote on five legislative
recommendations for inclusion in TWIA's 2026 Biennial Report to the
90th Texas Legislature (2027 session).

The five proposed legislative recommendations:

1. TIC § 2210.575(e) — ELIMINATE OR MAKE OPTIONAL the requirement that
   all TWIA suits be presided over by an MDL panel-appointed judge.
   TWIA's stated rationale: No litigant has ever formally requested
   MDL appointment in 12+ years the provision has been law.
   Significance: Policyholders would lose guaranteed access to
   specialized judges experienced in TWIA storm claim disputes.

2. FORTIFIED Home Construction — Consider feasibility of and public
   funding for FORTIFIED home construction and mitigation programs.

3. E&S Lines Tax — Consider imposing a surplus lines insurer tax to
   fund TWIA's Catastrophe Reserve Trust Fund.

4. Minimum Required Funding Restoration — Require TWIA to fund at
   1:100 PML each season and assess member companies annually for
   reinsurance above 1:50 PML (partially reversing HB 3689).

5. Depopulation / Additional Tort Reform — Additional structural reforms.

Meeting format: Austin + teleconference.

--- POST #1 — Coastal Flooding, Corpus Christi / Galveston ---

Flooding from last week's storms is still affecting parts of the Texas
coast. The National Weather Service in Corpus Christi issued Flood
Watches for Nueces County on October 1 and October 2. Streets in
Robstown flooded — Nueces Street, Mesquite Street, Garcia Street, and
West Avenue J. Galveston County is under a Flood Watch through Thursday.
The Coastal Bend barrier islands were under a Coastal Flood Advisory
through October 6. [1] [2] [3]

When flood and wind damage arrive together, policyholders face a claim
decision that was made without them.

Most standard homeowner's policies exclude flood damage entirely.
National Flood Insurance Program policies cover flood but not wind.
When a storm delivers both, adjusters decide which label applies to
which damage. That decision belongs to the carrier, not the homeowner.

NFIP policies give policyholders 60 days from the date of loss to file
a proof of loss. That window opened when the damage happened — not when
the adjuster arrived and not when the estimate was delivered.

The people dealing with flood damage in Nueces County, Galveston County,
and the Coastal Bend need their own documentation before anything is
cleaned or moved. The adjuster's visit is the carrier's documentation.
The photographs and video the homeowner takes are theirs.

--- POST #2 — TWIA Board Meeting Tomorrow (Oct 8) ---

Tomorrow, TWIA's board of directors meets to vote on what it will
recommend to the Texas Legislature in its 2026 Biennial Report. One
of those recommendations could eliminate a protection that has been in
Texas insurance law for more than twelve years. [1]

Under Texas Insurance Code Section 2210.575(e), TWIA lawsuits must be
heard by a judge assigned through the multi-district litigation process.
MDL judges have experience with insurance disputes. They have likely
already presided over dozens of TWIA claim cases from prior storms.

TWIA is proposing to make that requirement optional or eliminate it
entirely. Their stated reason is that no litigant has ever formally
requested MDL judge appointment under the current rule. That may be
because most TWIA cases settle before the question arises.

If the board votes to include this in the Biennial Report, it becomes a
formal TWIA legislative recommendation to the 90th Legislature in 2027.

What that means for policyholders: future TWIA disputes could go before
judges with no background in how TWIA adjusts claims, how it prices
repairs, or how it has litigated coverage in prior storms. The carrier
litigates these cases constantly. The homeowner usually does not.

The vote has not happened. The meeting is tomorrow.

[1] TWIA Board Meeting Notice, October 8, 2026:
https://www.twia.org/twia-board-to-meet-on-budget-legislative-recommendations/
