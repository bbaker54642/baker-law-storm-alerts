People in Horizon City are weeks into drying out, and the hard part of the claim is still ahead of them. El Paso County says more than 190 homes in the Lakeway and Mountain Shadow communities were damaged by floodwaters after severe storms in October. [1] The county declared a local disaster on October 27 and later extended it by 45 days. [2] A state disaster declaration remains in effect. [1] The Governor asked the Small Business Administration for a federal disaster declaration on November 11, and his office now reports the request was approved for El Paso and Hudspeth counties. [1][3] Damage estimates are still being assessed.

I've seen what a fast flood inspection looks like. The adjuster measures the water line and writes for what is below it. Water doesn't stop at the line. It wicks up drywall and into insulation. It sits under flooring and inside wall cavities long after the surface feels dry. It gets into wiring, outlets, cabinet bases and ductwork. Soil under a slab that stayed saturated can move later. An estimate written from what shows on the day of the inspection leaves all of that out.

People are entitled to an estimate that covers the whole loss, including the damage nobody can see from the doorway. A disaster loan is not a claim payment. It's debt. Horizon City shouldn't have to borrow what a carrier left off the estimate.
