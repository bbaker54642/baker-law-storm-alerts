TWIA 2027 RATES CONFIRMED FLAT — NO TDI DISAPPROVAL (Oct 15 Deadline)

Tomorrow, October 15, is TDI's statutory deadline for the Commissioner
to act on TWIA's 2027 rate filing (TDI Docket Nos. 2872 and 2873).
As of October 14, no Commissioner disapproval order has been announced.

Under Texas Insurance Code and TWIA's published rate timeline, a 0%
(no-change) filing that is not disapproved becomes effective without
formal Commissioner approval. Because TWIA filed 0% — not an increase —
TDI approval is not a legal prerequisite. The 2027 rate is effectively
confirmed absent a last-minute Commissioner action on October 15.

Context: TWIA filed 0% because its 2026 Rate Adequacy Analysis found
current rates adequate by approximately 9% (residential) and 4%
(commercial), driven by lower reinsurance costs from HB 3689 (2025).
HB 3689 reduced TWIA's required minimum funding reserve from a
1-in-100 to a 1-in-50 year probable maximum loss standard — cutting
reinsurance costs approximately 46% and eliminating the rate pressure.

What flat rates mean: TWIA rates are not increasing for 2027. The
statutory funding floor for policyholders in a catastrophic storm
is also not increasing from the reduced 1-in-50 year level.

--- POST #1 — NFIP Edouard Proof-of-Loss Deadline (URGENT) ---

Tropical Storm Edouard made landfall on September 1, 2026. [1] More
than six weeks later, no presidential major disaster declaration has
been issued for the affected Texas counties. [2]

That matters for one reason: the National Flood Insurance Program
proof-of-loss deadline.

Under NFIP policies, policyholders have 60 days from the date of loss
to submit a signed, sworn proof of loss. The 60-day clock started
September 1. That deadline is approximately October 31, 2026.

FEMA sometimes extends this deadline following a major disaster
declaration. No extension has been announced for Edouard. [3] Without
a declaration or a separate FEMA administrative action, no automatic
extension applies.

The proof-of-loss form is not the same as the initial claim report.
It is a sworn statement of the full amount owed, signed by the
policyholder, itemizing each category of loss. It must be complete
and submitted on time. A carrier can deny payment in full if it is late.

Filing a proof of loss while the adjuster's visit is still pending is
not unusual. File based on what you know. A supplemental proof of loss
can follow when the estimate arrives. The first one must be on time.

The deadline does not move because the carrier has not responded.
It does not move because the adjuster has not finished. It runs from
September 1.

--- POST #2 — TWIA 0% Rate for 2027 ---

TWIA's 2027 rates are staying where they are. Today is the Texas
Department of Insurance's deadline for the Commissioner to act on
TWIA's rate filing. No disapproval order has been announced. [1]
Under Texas law, a 0% rate filing that is not disapproved becomes
effective without formal action.

This is the second consecutive year TWIA has filed for no rate change.
The reason both times is the same: lower reinsurance costs tied to
House Bill 3689, which the 2025 Legislature passed to reduce TWIA's
required financial reserve from a 1-in-100 year to a 1-in-50 year
storm standard. [2]

Flat rates and adequate protection are different things.

The funding cushion TWIA is required to maintain before a major
hurricane has been reduced significantly by that statutory change.
The reserve covers what the model projects for a 1-in-50 year storm.
The prior requirement covered a 1-in-100 year storm. That is not a
technical distinction. It determines how much money is available when
a major hurricane makes landfall on the Texas coast.

TWIA rates did not go up. The statutory floor for the reserve that
backs TWIA policyholders in a catastrophic storm did not go up either.
Those two facts are connected.
