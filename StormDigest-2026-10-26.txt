FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1:
---
FEMA has denied Individual Assistance for all 30 Texas counties that Governor Abbott requested coverage for after the July 2026 storms.

The storms hit Hill Country and southwest Texas between July 12 and August 4, 2026, damaging homes across Kerr, Bandera, Gillespie, Uvalde, and 26 other counties. Governor Abbott requested a federal disaster declaration to unlock direct grants to homeowners and renters for uninsured losses. FEMA's answer: the damage was "not of such severity and magnitude to warrant designation of the individual assistance program." [1]

No FEMA grants for the people who lost the most.

What FEMA approved is Public Assistance: more than $135 million for government and nonprofit infrastructure. PA fixes roads, water systems, and public buildings. It does not pay a single dollar to any individual homeowner. [2]

Texas has 30 days to appeal the denial. The Governor's office has stated it is reviewing the decision.

For July 2026 storm survivors in those counties: the SBA physical damage loan application deadline is November 2, 2026 — one week from today. SBA disaster loans are available regardless of the IA denial. They are loans, not grants, but they are the only federal program open to individuals in these counties right now. [3]

Private insurance claims and SBA applications are not mutually exclusive. File both.

FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 2:
---
Tropical Storm Arthur made landfall near Matagorda Bay on June 17, 2026, with 45 mph sustained winds. Four people died. [1]

It has been four months. People whose properties flooded or suffered wind damage are now hearing final answers from their insurance companies.

Four months post-landfall is when the real fight starts. Carriers have made initial payments, conducted reinspections, and are now closing files. Roof damage disputes are common at this stage. Wind-versus-flood arguments are common. Actual cash value depreciation disputes — where the carrier pays less than what it costs to replace what you lost — are common at this stage.

In Matagorda County and along the coastal bend, many policyholders carry TWIA wind coverage and separate NFIP flood coverage. That means two separate claims, two separate dispute processes, and two separate deadlines.

TWIA policyholders generally have one year from the date of damage to file a claim. For June 17 losses, that deadline is June 17, 2027. The TWIA dispute process runs under Chapter 2210 of the Texas Insurance Code. [2] Private carrier claims are subject to the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act, with different deadlines.

If your Arthur claim has been denied or you believe you were underpaid, the deadline has not passed. The time to act is now — not when the anniversary is two weeks away.

[1] CBS News, "Tropical Storm Arthur makes landfall on Texas Gulf Coast," June 2026. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tropical-storm-arthur-first-hurricane-season-texas-gulf-coast/
[2] Texas Insurance Code § 2210.573 (TWIA one-year claim filing deadline).
