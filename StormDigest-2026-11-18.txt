--- DRAFT POSTS ---
DRAFT 1: Horizon City flood (confidence HIGH; replaces the unverified draft in the Nov. 17 digest)

People in Horizon City are almost a month into cleaning up, and for many of them the claim is only starting. Severe storms hit El Paso County from October 23 to 26. [1] El Paso County says floodwater damaged more than 190 homes in the Lakeway and Mountain Shadow communities. [2] County officials said the water was chest high in some homes and left several feet of mud behind. [3] The county declared a disaster on October 27 and the Governor followed on October 29. [2] The Small Business Administration has now issued a disaster declaration covering El Paso and Hudspeth counties. [1] The SBA says the deadline to return physical damage applications is January 12, 2027. [1] Damage estimates are still being assessed.

I've seen what a fast flood inspection looks like. The adjuster finds the water line and writes for what sits below it. Water doesn't stop there. It wicks up drywall and soaks insulation. It stays under flooring and inside wall cavities after the surface feels dry. It gets into wiring, outlets, cabinet bases and ductwork. Mud gets into everything the water touched. Soil that stayed saturated under a slab can move months later. An estimate built on what shows that day leaves those things out.

People are entitled to have the whole loss counted, including what nobody can see from the doorway. An SBA loan is not a claim payment. It's debt. Horizon City shouldn't have to borrow what a carrier left off the estimate.
