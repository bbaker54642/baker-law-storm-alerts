EVENT DATE: August 13, 2026 (federal court filing)
QUALIFIER: Galveston County policyholder class — qualifying coastal county

SUMMARY:
Three Galveston-area homeowners filed a federal RICO lawsuit against the Texas
Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA) on August 13, 2026 in the US District Court,
Southern District of Texas. The lawsuit alleges field adjuster estimates for Hurricane
Beryl damage were subsequently reduced by remote reviewers who never visited the
properties, without the policyholders' knowledge and while retaining the original
adjuster's name on the documents. One estimate dropped from nearly $91,000 to
approximately $30,800 (66% reduction). TWIA reported paying approximately $336 million
across 34,000+ Beryl claims; attorneys allege hundreds of millions were wrongfully
withheld. Plaintiffs seek class-action certification.

Three Galveston-area homeowners sued TWIA in federal court on August 13, under the
RICO statute.[1] I've followed this filing closely.

Here is what they allege. A field adjuster visited each property after Hurricane Beryl,
inspected the damage, and wrote a repair estimate. A remote reviewer who never set foot
on the property then accessed the file and reduced the numbers. One homeowner's estimate
dropped from nearly $91,000 to roughly $30,800 - a 66 percent cut.[2] The estimate
sent to that homeowner still bore the original field adjuster's name.

TWIA reported paying approximately $336 million across more than 34,000 Beryl
claims.[2] The attorneys in this case allege the actual amount owed was hundreds of
millions higher, and that the reduction practice was systematic.

The plaintiffs are seeking class-action certification. If the court grants it, thousands
of Beryl policyholders could be included in the case.

RICO is a federal racketeering statute. Courts apply it to organized, systematic fraud
- not one-off mistakes. The attorneys are arguing that this was not individual error.
It was a structured practice that reduced what policyholders received without their
knowledge.

The storm hit in 2024. The damage your adjuster documented was real. This lawsuit is
about what happened to those documented numbers before they became your payment.
