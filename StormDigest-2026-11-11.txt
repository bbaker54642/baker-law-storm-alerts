FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1:
---
The South Texas flooding that began November 8 has resolved. Flash flood warnings covered Kleberg, Nueces, Jim Wells, Duval, and San Patricio counties. Coastal flood advisories remained in effect for Aransas, Nueces, San Patricio, Refugio, and Calhoun counties through November 10. [1]

For people who had water enter their structure during this event: the claims process is not waiting for you to finish cleaning up. Contact your TWIA carrier and your flood insurance carrier now. File both claims if water entered your structure, regardless of how you think it got in. [2]

Water entry on the Texas coast typically produces a coverage dispute. The wind carrier says it was flood. The flood carrier says wind created the opening that let the rain in. That dispute is not resolved at the time you file. It is resolved later, often after the adjustment period, when carriers have already characterized your damage. [2]

Your NFIP policy requires a proof of loss within 60 days of the date of loss. For this event, that clock started November 8. The deadline falls around January 7, 2027. That deadline does not wait for the coverage dispute to be resolved. [3]

The documentation you created during the event is what carriers examine when they determine how much to pay. Keep it. [2]

FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 2:
---
The TWIA board voted on November 10, 2026 to borrow up to $45 million from JPMorgan Chase to pay lingering Hurricane Beryl claims. [1] TWIA's estimated Hurricane Beryl losses were approximately $535 million as of September 30, 2026. [1]

For policyholders who still have an open or disputed Beryl claim: TWIA just confirmed in public that it has the money to pay. Having the money and paying what a claim is worth are different questions. [1]

TWIA's spokesman said the association has ample financial capacity to pay all losses covered by its Beryl policies. That statement refers to TWIA's ability to pay, not to whether TWIA agrees on the amount your claim is worth. [1]

TWIA covers wind damage. Hurricane Beryl caused both wind and flood damage in the same structures. Where wind created an opening that allowed rain or surge to enter, those losses fall under the TWIA wind policy. TWIA adjusters characterize damage to minimize wind-covered loss. That characterization is not the final word on what TWIA owes. [2]

If TWIA paid your Beryl claim and you believe the payment was too low, the payment does not close your options. The appraisal provision in your TWIA policy remains available. [2]
