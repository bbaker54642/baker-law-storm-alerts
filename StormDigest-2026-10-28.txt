FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1 (FINAL WARNING):
---
October 31 is three days away. If Tropical Storm Edouard flooded your property on September 1, 2026, that is the deadline to file a signed, sworn proof of loss with your flood insurance carrier.

No FEMA presidential disaster declaration has been issued for Edouard — 57 days after landfall. No extension of the standard NFIP 60-day proof-of-loss window has been announced. [1]

A proof of loss is not a preliminary notice. It is not a phone call to your agent. It is a signed, sworn document with complete supporting documentation — every room, every item, every repair estimate, every photo. Your carrier does not have to pay if that document is not filed on time. The requirement is a condition of coverage, and carriers enforce it. [2]

The deadline is end of business Friday.

If you have not filed, file now. If you believe you filed but are not certain the document is complete, call your carrier today and confirm. If your carrier has already paid a partial amount and you believe the payment was too low, your proof of loss should reflect the full amount of your claimed loss — not just what was already offered.

People who wait for a declaration that may never come will lose benefits they are entitled to.

FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 2:
---
Today is the last day to appeal FEMA's denial of Individual Assistance for the July 2026 Texas storms.

FEMA denied Individual Assistance for all 30 counties Governor Abbott requested after floods struck Hill Country and southwest Texas between July 12 and August 4, 2026. The denial came October 21-22. Texas has 30 days to appeal. That 30-day window closes today. [1]

As of October 27, the appeal had not been filed. [2]

If the appeal is not filed today, the denial is final. That means no FEMA grants to individual homeowners, renters, or small businesses in Kerr, Bandera, Uvalde, Gillespie, Val Verde, and 25 other counties — for any uninsured losses from those storms.

The SBA physical damage loan deadline is November 2, 2026 — this Sunday. That is the only federal recovery program still open to individuals in those counties. SBA disaster loans are loans, not grants. They still require an application. An SBA loan and a private insurance claim are not mutually exclusive. [3]

If you have uninsured losses from the July 2026 storms, apply for an SBA loan before November 2. The application is at disasterloanassistance.sba.gov.

If your private insurance claim from those storms has been denied or underpaid, that deadline is separate from the SBA application deadline.
