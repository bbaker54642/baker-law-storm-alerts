--- POST #1 — Polo/Cold Front Flooding, Coastal Counties ---

This past week, the National Weather Service issued Flood Watches for
eight coastal Texas counties: Brazoria, Calhoun, Galveston, Jackson,
Matagorda, Nueces, San Patricio, and Victoria. Parts of Southeast Texas
took more than seven inches of rain in a single night. More than 14,000
customers lost power across the state. [1] [2]

When flood and wind damage arrive together, the claim process gets
complicated fast.

Most standard homeowner's policies exclude flood damage entirely.
National Flood Insurance Program policies cover flood but not wind.
When a storm produces both, the question of which policy covers which
damage is not answered by the storm. It is answered by the adjuster.

That is not a neutral process. Insurance carriers and NFIP adjusters
have financial incentives to characterize damage in ways that reduce
their exposure. The homeowner does not typically get a say in which
label gets applied to the water line on their wall.

Under NFIP policies, policyholders have 60 days from the date of loss
to file a proof of loss. That deadline runs from when the damage
occurred, not from when the adjuster shows up or the estimate arrives.

Before anything is moved or cleaned, photograph and video the damage
with timestamps. The first record in a claim dispute is usually the
most important one.

--- POST #2 — TX House Insurance Committee Hearing ---

Today, the Texas House Insurance Committee is holding its interim
hearing at the Capitol in Austin. The committee is looking at the
insurance decisions that shape what coverage is available on the Texas
coast and what happens when people try to make a claim. [1]

On the agenda: the TWIA funding change from last session. House Bill
3689 reduced TWIA's minimum financial reserve from surviving a
one-in-100-year storm to surviving a one-in-50-year storm. That change
cut TWIA's reinsurance costs by roughly 46 percent, which is why rates
did not increase this year. It also means the cushion available when a
major hurricane makes landfall is smaller than it used to be.

Also on the agenda: the insurance appraisal process, and how insurance
companies use it. Under Texas law, policyholders can invoke appraisal
when they dispute an insurer's estimate of damages. Senate Bill 458
addresses how that process works in practice. The carriers do not
always use it in ways that benefit the policyholder.

The committee hears testimony from carriers, regulators, and sometimes
homeowners. Those hearings shape the legislative priorities that come
back in January. What gets raised today can become law two years from
now.

--- POST #3 — TWIA MDL Judge Vote (Oct 8 Board Meeting) ---

TWIA is holding a board meeting on October 8 to consider eliminating
a rule that affects how policyholders pursue disputed claims in court.

The current rule, under Texas Insurance Code Section 2210.575(e),
requires that all TWIA lawsuits be presided over by a judge appointed
through the MDL system. MDL, or multi-district litigation, is a process
used to coordinate similar cases and assign them to judges with
experience in that category of dispute.

For TWIA policyholders, that meant their case was heard by a judge who
had likely presided over other TWIA storm cases and understood how TWIA
adjusts claims, prices repairs, and litigates coverage disputes.

If TWIA's board votes to eliminate or make this rule optional, new
TWIA suits may be routed to judges without that background. Individual
policyholders bringing a claim dispute would face a carrier that
litigates these cases constantly, before a judge who may not.

The MDL coordination mechanism also helps identify patterns. When
hundreds of claims from the same storm are handled in one court system,
it becomes easier to spot whether carriers are systematically
underpaying a specific category of damage. That visibility becomes
harder when cases scatter across different courts.

The vote has not happened yet. This is what TWIA's board is considering.
