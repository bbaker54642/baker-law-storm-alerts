--- POST #1 — TDI Transparency Tool ---

Texas just built a window into something insurance companies would
rather you not see.

On September 25, 2026, the Texas Department of Insurance launched
a new public transparency tool under House Bill 2067. For the first
time, anyone can search insurance carrier declination, non-renewal,
and cancellation notices by ZIP code and policy type. TDI has uploaded
more than 350,000 notices through June 2026, with quarterly updates
going forward. [1]

This matters in coastal Texas. When a carrier decides to stop writing
new policies or non-renew existing ones in Aransas, Nueces, or Galveston
counties, that is a choice they make. It affects your ability to get
coverage, get a mortgage, or sell your home. Until now, those decisions
happened without any way for the public to see the pattern.

The tool changes that. A homeowner wondering why they received a
non-renewal letter can now check whether their carrier is pulling back
from an entire area or just from their property. A county can track
which insurers are withdrawing from its ZIP codes over time.

The TDI tool does not explain why carriers make these decisions. But
it makes the pattern visible. That is the first step to holding them
accountable.

Search tool available at tdi.texas.gov.

[1] Insurance Journal, Sept. 25, 2026:
https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southcentral/2026/09/25/886910.htm

--- POST #2 — Edouard DR Still Pending ---

Four weeks ago, Tropical Storm Edouard hit southeast Texas. Homeowners
in Harris, Jefferson, Liberty, and Orange counties are still waiting
for the federal government to act.

Governor Abbott requested a presidential major disaster declaration after
Edouard made landfall on September 1, 2026. Senators Cruz and Cornyn
have both expressed support for the request. As of today, no presidential
declaration has been issued. [1]

That matters in practical terms. Without a presidential major disaster
declaration, FEMA Individual Assistance is not available. No temporary
housing grants. No home repair grants. The only federal disaster loans
open are SBA loans from a separate administrative declaration covering
August storms. [2]

Local governments have issued their own declarations in Jefferson County,
Port Arthur, Hardin County, and Polk County. But local declarations do
not unlock federal aid to individual households.

What that means right now: the people who had Edouard damage are
relying almost entirely on their insurance policies. Private insurance
is their primary lifeline. That is exactly why it matters when carriers
dispute, delay, or underpay claims.

One deadline is already on the clock. NFIP policyholders who had
flood damage from Edouard have approximately until November 1, 2026
to file a proof of loss. That window does not move.
