FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1 (URGENT):
---
October 31 is the deadline to file a flood insurance proof of loss if Tropical Storm Edouard flooded your property on September 1, 2026.

Edouard made landfall along the Southeast Texas coast with 60 mph sustained winds and dropped more than 20 inches of rain across Hardin and Tyler counties. [1] Jefferson, Hardin, Tyler, Polk, Liberty, and Chambers counties all issued local disaster declarations. [2]

Under the National Flood Insurance Program, policyholders have 60 days from the date of loss to submit a signed, sworn proof of loss. For September 1 losses, that window closes October 31. As of today, no FEMA presidential disaster declaration has been issued for Edouard, and no NFIP extension has been announced. [3]

If you miss that deadline, your carrier does not have to pay your claim. The proof of loss requirement is not a formality. It is a condition of coverage, and carriers enforce it. FEMA can authorize extensions, but they have not done so here.

If you have an NFIP policy and your home or business flooded on September 1, file your proof of loss before October 31. That means a complete, signed, sworn document with all supporting documentation. Not a preliminary notice. Not a phone call to your agent. The signed document.

People who wait for the declaration that may never come will lose benefits they are entitled to.

FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 2:
---
AIG Property Casualty has filed for a 15.2% homeowners insurance rate increase in Texas under the state's file-and-use law.

That filing does not require the Texas Department of Insurance to approve it before it takes effect. [1]

Ten days ago, TDI reported that Texas homeowners rates are averaging a 4.3% decrease across filings since July 1. AIG is asking for more than three times that amount in the opposite direction. State Farm is also moving against the trend, having filed a 4% increase effective November 1. [2]

Texas has 26 homeowners rate decreases and 5 increases filed over the same period. The increases are concentrated at a few large national carriers; the decreases are spread across regional and specialty writers. The average conceals a wide range of individual outcomes.

Policyholders with AIG homeowners coverage should check their renewal date. If your policy renews after this rate takes effect, your premium will reflect the increase without separate notice beyond your renewal declaration. You are not entitled to a mid-term rate reduction. If your renewal is approaching, compare your options now.

What TDI has done is enforce Commissioner's Bulletin B-0008-26, which requires insurers to account for FORTIFIED roof upgrades in their rate calculations. If you have a FORTIFIED roof certification and your carrier is not discounting your premium for it, that is a filing that TDI can review. [3]
