Hurricane Isaias (2026) — COMPLETE TRACK SUMMARY:

- Formed as Tropical Storm Isaias on October 7 in the SW Gulf/Bay of
  Campeche (~22.0°N, 94.1°W), 40 mph winds at time of formation.
- Became the 2026 Atlantic season's FIRST HURRICANE on October 8 as
  it crossed into the eastern Gulf and rapidly intensified.
- Peak intensity: Category 3 (reached during Gulf crossing).
- Slightly weakened before landfall.
- LANDFALL: Category 2 hurricane near Destin, Florida Panhandle,
  Friday October 9, 2026.
- Dissipated Sunday October 11 when absorbed by another weather system.

SIGNIFICANCE: Isaias ended the longest hurricane drought in the modern
satellite era for the 2026 Atlantic season. Eight named storms before
Isaias had all remained below hurricane strength. Isaias briefly reached
Category 3, making it the season's first major hurricane.

TEXAS IMPACT: Texas avoided direct impacts. The storm's forecast track
held to the northeast corridor (Louisiana-Mississippi-Alabama-Florida),
keeping Texas on the far western periphery. However, Isaias produced
INDIRECT EFFECTS on Texas qualifying coastal counties (see Section 2).

No tropical cyclone watches or warnings were issued for Texas.

Current status (Oct 12): No active tropical systems threatening Texas.
Gulf of Mexico is clear. Atlantic basin quiet. One low-probability
disturbance (~20% formation odds) near the Cabo Verde Islands — no
Gulf threat.

--- POST #1 — Hurricane Isaias Coastal Flooding, Coastal Bend ---

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin on the Florida Panhandle
on October 9 as a Category 2 hurricane. [1] The storm did not hit
Texas directly. But it sent coastal flooding to the Texas coast. The
National Weather Service in Corpus Christi issued Coastal Flood
Advisories for Aransas, Nueces, San Patricio, Refugio, and Calhoun
counties running through October 13. [2] The city of Corpus Christi
urged beachgoers to stay off Padre Island due to dangerous surf. [3]

When a hurricane passes east of the Texas coast, the flooding it
leaves behind is real. The question is which policy covers it.

Most homeowner's policies exclude flood damage. That exclusion does
not pause when the flood comes from a hurricane's outer circulation
rather than its direct path. Carriers look at the cause of the water,
not the severity of the storm. Water from tidal surge or elevated
seas is flood. Under most policies, it is excluded.

NFIP policyholders have 60 days from the date of loss to file a proof
of loss. That window opened when the water arrived in Aransas, Nueces,
San Patricio, Refugio, and Calhoun counties — not when the adjuster
scheduled a visit.

Before anything is moved or cleaned, photograph and video the damage
with timestamps. The adjuster's visit documents what the carrier needs.
The homeowner's own documentation is the only record they control.

--- POST #2 — TWIA Votes to Recommend Eliminating MDL Judge Requirement ---

On October 8, TWIA's board of directors voted to recommend to the
Texas Legislature that the requirement for MDL judges in TWIA lawsuits
be eliminated or made optional. [1]

Under Texas Insurance Code Section 2210.575(e), every TWIA lawsuit
must be presided over by a judge appointed by the judicial panel on
multi-district litigation. MDL judges have experience with insurance
disputes. They have likely already seen how TWIA adjusts claims, how
it prices repairs, and how it litigates coverage disputes.

TWIA told its board that no litigant has ever formally requested an
MDL judge appointment in the 12-plus years the rule has been in effect.
That may be accurate. It does not explain what the rule has prevented.
When a carrier knows a specialized judge is watching, that knowledge
is part of the system.

The board's vote places this recommendation in TWIA's 2026 Biennial
Report to the 90th Texas Legislature. The 90th Legislature convenes in
2027. To become law, the Legislature must vote to change the statute.

What the vote means right now: TWIA has formally asked its own oversight
Legislature to reduce the procedural protection available to policyholders
who sue TWIA over disputed claims.

That is the choice on the table.

https://www.twia.org/twia-board-reviews-budget-advances-legislative-recommendations/
