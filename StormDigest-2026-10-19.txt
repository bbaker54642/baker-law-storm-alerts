FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1:
---
Between October 15 and 18, a cold front drove gale-force winds and coastal flooding across five Texas Gulf Coast counties: Aransas, Nueces, San Patricio, Calhoun, and Matagorda.

NOAA's National Weather Service office in Corpus Christi issued gale warnings from Baffin Bay to the Matagorda Ship Channel. Coastal flood advisories warned of 1.0 to 1.5 feet of inundation above normal tide levels in Aransas, Nueces, and Calhoun counties. The advisories ran from October 15 through October 18. [1]

That kind of flooding reaches structures — garages, slab foundations, storage buildings, fishing equipment, boat lifts, and the lower courses of pier-and-beam homes. Whether insurance covers it depends on which policies are in force. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover rising water. Flood insurance under the National Flood Insurance Program does, but only if the policy was in effect before the flood began — coverage cannot be purchased once a storm is named or a watch is posted. [2]

If your property in one of these five counties experienced water intrusion during this event, document everything with dated photos, save all repair estimates, and report to your carrier in writing. Do not assume a denial is final.

[1] NWS Corpus Christi, coastal flood advisories and gale warning, Oct 15–18, 2026. https://www.weather.gov/crp/
[2] FEMA NFIP policy conditions: https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance

State Farm Lloyds has filed a 4% statewide homeowners insurance rate increase with the Texas Department of Insurance, effective November 1, 2026.

The filing reveals that State Farm's actuaries calculated a 10.9% rate need — the company filed for less than half of what its own data justifies. Under Texas's file-and-use law, that rate takes effect without prior TDI approval. [1]

This comes ten days after TDI announced that Texas homeowners rates are trending down 4.3% overall for 2026. State Farm is moving in the opposite direction. [2]

Texas coastal policyholders should pay attention to this. State Farm is one of the largest writers of residential property insurance in the state. A 4% increase on November 1 means policies renewing after that date will cost more. People who have already received renewal offers should check whether their quoted premium reflects the new rate.

There is a bigger number behind this filing: 10.9%. That is what State Farm says it actually needs. A company that files far below its stated actuarial need is a company managing short-term optics while reserving the right to file again. The 4% filed today is likely not the last increase you will see from this carrier.

If your homeowners policy is up for renewal in the next 60 days, compare your options now — before the rate takes effect.
