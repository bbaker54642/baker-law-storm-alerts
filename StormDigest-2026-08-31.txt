The August 28 windstorm in San Antonio produced measured gusts of 77 mph
at San Antonio International Airport and knocked out power for more than
104,000 CPS Energy customers.[1] I've seen what storms like this set in motion.

City inspectors surveyed 75 properties over the weekend, including 71 homes
and four commercial structures.[2] Two commercial buildings required emergency
demolition after structural assessments.[2] Two deaths are being investigated
as storm-related.[2]

When a storm produces this kind of documented structural damage, the insurance
process that follows is predictable. Adjusters arrive with wind speed data,
policy language, and a checklist. They look for ways to separate wind damage
from rain intrusion. They look for pre-existing conditions. They look for
depreciation arguments on roofing systems and siding.

Bexar County homeowners hold policies subject to Texas Department of Insurance
rules. Those rules require insurers to acknowledge claims, request
documentation, and issue payment or denial letters within fixed timeframes.
What carriers choose to do within those timeframes is a different matter.

A structural inspection report from a city engineer is a factual document. It
belongs in your claim file from the moment it exists. Adjusters know to use
those reports. Policyholders often do not.

The storm set the damage. The adjuster decides what gets paid.
