FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 1:
---
Texas filed an appeal of FEMA's denial of Individual Assistance for the July 2026 Hill Country and southwest Texas storms. The appeal was approved October 30, 2026. [1]

Twenty of the 30 originally requested counties are now covered for IA: Bandera, Bexar, Comal, Crockett, Dimmit, Frio, Gillespie, Kendall, Kerr, Kimble, Kinney, La Salle, Maverick, Medina, Real, Schleicher, Sutton, Uvalde, Val Verde, and Zavala. [1]

FEMA Individual Assistance can pay for temporary housing, emergency home repairs, and uninsured personal property losses. It does not cover losses your insurance already paid. [2]

Applications are open now. Apply at disasterassistance.gov, call 1-800-621-3362, or use the FEMA app. There is a registration deadline. The current deadline is listed at disasterassistance.gov. [2]

Ten of the 30 originally requested counties were not included in the approval. Policyholders in those 10 counties have no IA path for these storms. The ten counties not approved have not been publicly identified yet. [1]

FEMA assistance and a private insurance claim are not mutually exclusive. Receiving FEMA assistance does not close your insurance claim or waive any right to dispute an underpayment. [2]

A new SBA physical damage loan window for homeowners and businesses in the 20 IA-covered counties is expected to open within days, following standard procedure after an IA designation. Watch sba.gov/disaster for the announcement and deadline. [3]

FACEBOOK POST — EVENT 2:
---
The October 31, 2026 deadline to file an NFIP proof of loss for Tropical Storm Edouard has passed. No FEMA presidential disaster declaration was issued for Edouard. No extension of the 60-day proof-of-loss window was announced. [1]

Under the National Flood Insurance Program, a signed, sworn proof of loss is a condition of coverage. Carriers are not required to pay a claim without it. That requirement did not change when the deadline passed. [2]

For policyholders who missed the deadline: the standard claim path is now closed. A late proof of loss may still be submitted to your carrier with a written explanation and a request for extension. FEMA has discretion to authorize late filings. Carriers routinely deny these requests. Some do not. [2]

For policyholders who filed on time but believe they were underpaid: the proof-of-loss deadline and a dispute over the payment amount are separate questions. Filing on time satisfies the condition of coverage. Whether what was paid was the right number is a different issue. [2]

For policyholders who filed, received a payment, and are satisfied: the deadline is behind them.

The result for each policyholder is different depending on where they were on October 31.

[1] FEMA disaster declarations. https://www.fema.gov/disaster/declarations
[2] FEMA NFIP policy conditions. https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance
